Correct fraction
outcomes/correct_fraction: correct final kernels divided by all eight rollouts. Higher is useful, but every point uses a different curriculum task and the samples were generated before that point's optimizer update.
Reward and speed
outcomes/mean_reward combines correctness and speed using the unchanged reward. outcomes/median_speedup_correct uses only correct kernels; above 1 means faster than their same-device reference. A failed or missing speed measurement is not replaced with zero. Training-task difficulty and the GPU switch at step 20 prevent a causal before/after comparison.
Loss
train/loss is the GRPO policy objective, not accuracy or supervised prediction error. It may be negative or fluctuate; it is not expected to decrease smoothly as different tasks arrive. P+R≤C

What per-turn RLM credit means

A trajectory can inspect, write, test and submit. GRPO calculates one advantage from its final reward. The same advantage trains each root reply, using the exact prompt that reply saw. REPL observations and child replies are not loss targets.

How to compare actual cost

Cost is allocated resources multiplied by time to a useful result, including startup and failed attempts. Under equal per-GPU pricing, two GPUs beat three only if they take less than 1.5 times as long. CPU/RAM charges, provider credits and storage change this threshold.

Colocation can reduce allocated GPU count but can also remove training/generation overlap. Offload needs host RAM and adds transfers. Quantization may help on smaller GPUs, but it changes numerical behavior and requires end-to-end compatibility checks. No migration or GPU benchmark was run for this review.

  1. Compile with CUDA devices hidden, without blocking inference.

2. Keep the current decisions clear

Reward: correctness and speed both matter
  • Valid source earns partial credit.
  • A compiled extension earns more credit.
  • A correct result earns correctness credit and a speed bonus.
  • Intermediate feedback gives information, not reward. Only the final submission is scored.

For a valid, compiled, correct submission:

r=0.7+0.1×clamp(speedup,0,3)

Speedup is reference time divided by kernel time. A value below 1 means the kernel is slower.

Filter: reject identical total rewards. Keep all-correct groups if their speed rewards differ. Timing noise can affect admission.

4. Learn the training loop

  1. Select a task. Put the reference in the Python REPL.
  2. Generate attempts. The model writes code and receives REPL output.
  3. Test a candidate. cuda_feedback(source) returns compiler and correctness feedback.
  4. Submit. The model sets the answer content and ready flag.
  5. Score. The verifier checks the final source and measures speed.
  6. Update. Prime compares rewards, filters groups, and updates LoRA parameters.
Turn, trajectory, group, batch, and step
Turn
One model response and its environment interaction.
Trajectory
One complete attempt, with one or more turns.
Group
Attempts at the same task. Training group size: 4.
Training batch
Accepted trajectories for an update. Configured size: 4.
Micro-batch
A smaller packed token unit. The trainer accumulates gradients across these units.
Optimizer step
An update to trainable parameters. It is not a REPL turn.
Async execution and GPU roles
  • One A100 runs vLLM inference.
  • Two A100s jointly train Laguna with expert parallelism.
  • Prime overlaps generation and training by default.
  • Group-relative scoring still needs the group results.
  • The inherited maximum policy-version lag is 8. This is a limit, not a constant delay.

Modal provides compute and storage. Prime coordinates training. Our environment defines interaction and reward.

GRPO intuition and pseudocode

Compare each attempt with other attempts on the same task. In the mean-centered form:

Ai=ri−r¯
  • Positive advantage favors the sampled actions.
  • Negative advantage discourages them.
  • Identical rewards give no relative reward signal.
repeat until the step or time limit:
    sample a group of trajectories
    verify each final submission
    compute total rewards, including speed
    if all total rewards are identical:
        reject the group
        continue within the time budget
    pack accepted trajectories into micro-batches
    accumulate gradients and update LoRA parameters
    synchronize inference weights
    save a checkpoint when scheduled

This is a conceptual loop. Prime overlaps generation and training and applies its configured loss corrections.

Checkpoint versus inference weight update
Checkpoint
Model state, optimizer, scheduler, and progress. Used to resume training.
Weight update
Transfers the current policy to inference. Not a substitute for a checkpoint.
Retention
Limits saved checkpoint count. It does not eliminate write cost or limit all other artifacts.

6. Research foundations and evaluation evidence

The references below distinguish methods and earlier influences already recorded in this project from newly identified related work. Citing a paper does not mean we implemented its complete system, reproduced its results, or verified a conference venue for every preprint.

How kernel and GRPO papers evaluate learning

Changing tasks during training is normal. Training reward, loss and entropy describe optimization. Evaluation asks a different question: how does each checkpoint perform on the same benchmark, or on comparable samples from an unchanged evaluation distribution? Our easy-first ordering changes the task mix, and our overview excludes deferred groups. Adjacent points are therefore not a controlled policy comparison.

KernelBench · ICML 2025
Ouyang et al. evaluate a common set of kernel workloads. Their fast_p is the fraction of tasks with a correct implementation whose reference/kernel runtime ratio exceeds speed threshold p. Correctness, timing baseline, hardware and sampling budget must accompany the number. Conference paper · metric definition.
Kevin · ICLR 2026
The cited arXiv v1 trains on 180 tasks and evaluates on 100 separate tasks: 20 reserved KernelBench tasks plus 80 newly constructed tasks. Its main comparison uses 16 parallel trajectories with eight refinement turns and distinguishes best@16 from avg@16. The headline 82% correctness is best-of-16; average trajectory correctness is 46%. Neither is our single-task eight-rollout rate. Venue · split · evaluation.
Dr. Kernel · ICML 2026
Liu et al. plot KernelBench Level-2 Fast@1 and Fast@1.2 against training steps, then compare models under a common H100 evaluation setup: eight samples per question, three turns for the main comparison. Last-turn and best-of-history results are reported separately; the stronger best-history figure is not final-submission-only performance. TRLOO and profiling rewards are their methods, not features already implemented here. Conference paper · setup · turn selection.
DAPO · NeurIPS 2025
Yu et al. distinguish training diagnostics from AIME evaluation accuracy. They repeat the evaluation set 32 times and report avg@32, not best-of-32. Thus a GRPO-family training run can encounter different training questions while its quality curve uses a consistent evaluation benchmark. Conference paper · evaluation settings.
TimeRLM · arXiv v2
Zumarraga et al. generate fresh training batches, then evaluate the resulting model on AnomalyXL, ARFBench and unseen clinical recordings. They report final-checkpoint performance, rather than treating every changing training batch as a held-out learning curve. This is direct related work for RLM reinforcement learning, but its time-series scores are not CUDA benchmarks. experiments and transfer.
CUDA Agent and stricter verification
CUDA Agent evaluates a separate KernelBench suite after AST-similarity filtering of training programs, and reports pass rate, faster rate and geometric mean speedup against both eager and compiled Torch. Its training algorithm is PPO with a critic, not our GRPO baseline. KernelBench-Verified adds hidden input-distribution checks, a TF32-enabled reference and memory measurement: a high score still depends on how correctness and baseline timing are defined. CUDA Agent §§3–4, Appendix A · KernelBench-Verified.

Notation matters: in fast_p@k, p is the speed threshold and k is the sampling budget. fast_1 does not mean pass@1. Also distinguish final submission, best kernel within a trajectory, and best across trajectories. Report the selection procedure and all allowed verifier/feedback access.

Evidence limitation: benchmark reuse for checkpoint selection is development feedback, not an untouched final test. Published protocols differ in contamination controls and selection budgets; they are examples to examine, not a blanket guarantee of clean generalization.
